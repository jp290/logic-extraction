## 1. TECHNIQUES & METHODS

### Salting chicken in advance
- **What it achieves:** Gets the flavour right, intensifies flavour
- **Conditions:** Apply to meat before cooking; applies to chicken specifically in this context
- [Context: chicken-rosemary-lemon dish, pre-cooking preparation]

### Hot pan with sufficient beef tallow
- **What it achieves:** Efficiently transfers heat from pan into the chicken
- **Conditions:** Pan should not be overwhelmed (heating ability not exceeded); enough fat/oil in bottom of pan, but not too much (excess causes boiling rather than frying)
- [Context: initial searing of chicken]

### Waiting for roast aroma before turning
- **What it signals:** "Once the right temperature is reached, the forming of roast aroma should be smell-able. This marks the point of turning the chickens."
- **What it prevents:** Flipping too soon causes chicken to get "rather cooked than 'fried'" which leads to too much moisture coming out
- [Context: searing chicken in pan]

### Waiting for roasting to come again before moving
- **What it achieves:** Once chickens are nicely coated (after second side seared), they can be moved out of the pan
- **What it prevents:** Moving chickens too much in the pan before proper roasting
- [Context: post-flip, during second-side searing]

### Deglazing with white wine
- **What it achieves:** Loosens stuck/burnt-on bits from bottom of pan
- **Method:** "Take a spill of white wine and 'throw' it in there. Let it boil a bit and just put it over the roasted chicken which is probably resting in a pod next to the stove."
- **Condition:** Use when there is too much build-up on the bottom of the pan
- [Context: after removing chicken, before cooking onions]

### Roasting onions with lid closed briefly
- **What it achieves:** "Onion's moisture comes out which fastens this quite a bit" (speeds up cooking)
- **Condition:** Add fat (beef tallow is best) first; close lid for a short period
- [Context: after deglazing, cooking onions]

### Adding lemon juice and rosemary at the end, with reduced heat
- **What it achieves:** Lightly steams rosemary; rosemary eaten "quite gently steamed" gives "a very grounded feeling + health effects, especially for the gut"
- **Method:** "Add lemon juice and stir again. This last step might do better with a bit less heat actually, especially as now the rosemary is added and just stirred into it and lightly steamed from the rest of the heat. A half closed lid or e.g. a fat screen for 30s-1min does the job very well already."
- **Sequence:** After chicken is added back and stirred; lemon juice added and stirred; then rosemary added and stirred
- [Context: finishing chicken-rosemary-lemon dish]

### "Always question what you're doing" (attributed to Marco Pierre White)
- **What it is:** "Reason about all the processes that are happening"
- **Application:** Constant mode during cooking; extends beyond cooking
- [Context: Interviewee's general approach]

### "Honest attention/focus on what is actually happening in the pan"
- **What it achieves:** Provides real-time feedback on cooking process
- **What to be most sensitive to:** "How much heat the pan has and how much fat is in the pan, because only with enough fat the pan can transfer the heat evenly into what's in the pan"
- [Context: Interviewee's internal monitoring during cooking]

### Reasoning about mistakes
- **Method:** "Try to understand it, but... not get crazy about it. Just try to learn a lesson and reason a bit what might've caused it and what I could do better"
- [Context: Interviewee's approach to errors]

## 2. KEY ENTITIES & DOMAIN CONCEPTS

### Beef tallow
- **Role:** "Standard cooking oil"; preferred fat for heat transfer
- **Function:** "Only with enough fat the pan can transfer the heat evenly into what's in the pan"
- [Context: Multiple references throughout]

### Marco Pierre White
- **Role:** Source of key principles: "conviction in his pan that things will not immediately burn" and "always question what you're doing"
- **Function:** Provided "the lens I needed to learn myself"; watching his videos was "extremely helpful"
- [Context: Interviewee's learning journey]

### The distinction between "cooking" and "frying" (or "roasting")
- **Key concept:** "One only make the food done but the other builds up proper flavour"
- **Implication:** Roast/fry first for flavor, then cook for doneness
- **What ruins it:** Closing lid completely builds up "a light amount of pressure in the pod, but which is enough to ruin the crispiness quite instantly"
- **When lid is acceptable:** "More to the end" at medium-to-low heat, for short duration (30s-1min)
- [Context: Interviewee's fundamental cooking philosophy]

### "Roast first, then go for doneness"
- **Philosophy:** "You always actually roast ingredients first and then go for done-ness"
- **Application:** Sequence is designed to "get it all to the degree it should be cooked without over cooking/roasting anything"
- [Context: General principle, demonstrated in chicken dish]

### Salt as a "spotlight" for aroma
- **Concept:** Salting at different stages "enhances the flavour of the aroma that it is hitting"
- **Practice:** Salt meat in advance; sometimes salt vegetables (broccoli for frying); salt when the roasting is coming
- [Context: Interviewee's explanation of "salting in layers"]

### "Grounded" cooking producing "whole" feeling
- **Outcome:** Food makes you feel whole when you eat it; not craving chips or sweets afterwards
- **Mechanism:** "All the fat & salt is seemingly plenty flavour"
- [Context: Interviewee's holistic approach to cooking]

## 3. MEASUREMENTS & QUANTITIES

### Heat setting: 8-9/10, possibly 10/10
- **What it prescribes:** "All this roasting should happen on 8-9/10 heat, maybe even 10/10"
- **Condition:** Depends on volume being cooked; rather add more oil to transfer heat than turn stove down
- [Context: General roasting instructions]

### Lid/fat screen duration: 30 seconds to 1 minute
- **What it prescribes:** For steaming rosemary at the end: "A half closed lid or e.g. a fat screen for 30s-1min does the job very well already"
- **Condition:** Medium-to-low heat; after all other ingredients are added
- [Context: Final step of chicken-rosemary-lemon dish]

### Fat quantity: "Enough" but not "too much"
- **What it prescribes:** "Enough fat/oil in the bottom of the pan (not too much as then it will also rather start boiling)"
- **Condition:** For efficient heat transfer without boiling the food
- [Context: Initial pan preparation]

## 4. TIMELINES & SEQUENCES

### Chicken-rosemary-lemon dish — full sequence

1. **Salt chicken in advance** (pre-cooking preparation)
2. **Heat pan with beef tallow** on high heat (8-9/10 or 10/10)
3. **Add chicken to hot pan** — do not flip too soon
4. **Wait for roast aroma** — signals when to turn chicken
5. **Turn chicken** — wait again for roasting to come before moving
6. **Remove chicken** once nicely coated on both sides; rest in pod next to stove
7. **Deglaze pan with white wine** if there's too much build-up; let it boil briefly; pour over resting chicken
8. **Add fat again** (beef tallow best) to pan
9. **Roast onions** — close lid briefly to release moisture and speed cooking
10. **Add chicken back** into pan with onions; stir
11. **Add lemon juice**; stir again
12. **Reduce heat** slightly
13. **Add rosemary**; stir in
14. **Cover with half-closed lid or fat screen** for 30 seconds to 1 minute
15. **Serve** with rice (e.g.)

[Context: Entire walkthrough of the dish]

### General sequence principle: Roast first, then doneness
- **Order:** Build flavor through roasting/frying first; then finish cooking to desired doneness
- **Lid use:** "More to the end" — closing lid completely is done late in process, at medium-to-low heat, for short duration
- [Context: Interviewee's fundamental philosophy]

### Mistake analysis sequence
1. Notice something went wrong
2. Try to understand what happened
3. Do not get crazy about it
4. Learn a lesson
5. Reason about what might have caused it
6. Determine what could be done better next time
- [Context: Interviewee's approach to errors]

## 1. DECISION LOGIC

**A. The Core Cooking Process (Chicken-Rosemary-Lemon)**
- IF you are cooking chicken: THEN salt it in advance to get the flavour right. `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`
- IF you are frying chicken in a pan: THEN use a "hood amount" of beef tallow to efficiently transfer heat from the pan into the chicken. `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`
- IF the pan is too hot (overwhelming its heating ability): THEN the chicken will get "rather cooked than 'fried'," leading to too much moisture leaving the chicken. `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`
- IF you flip the chicken too soon: THEN the chicken will get "rather cooked than 'fried'," leading to too much moisture leaving the chicken. `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`
- IF steam/heat is trapped in the pan (e.g., with a lid): THEN the chicken will get "rather cooked than 'fried'," leading to too much moisture leaving the chicken. `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`
- IF you are frying: THEN you need conviction in your pan that things will not immediately burn. This requires enough fat/oil in the bottom of the pan. `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`
- BUT IF there is too much fat/oil: THEN the food will start "rather boiling" instead of frying. `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`
- IF the right temperature is reached: THEN you should smell the forming of roast aroma. This marks the point of turning the chicken. `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`
- IF you have turned the chicken: THEN wait for the roasting to come again before moving the chicken too much in the pan. `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`
- IF there is too much build-up (fond) on the bottom of the pan: THEN it is well advised to take a spill of white wine and 'throw' it in. Let it boil a bit, then put it over the roasted chicken. `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`
- IF you are roasting onions: THEN it is a good idea to close the lid for a bit so that the onion's moisture comes out, which fastens the roasting. `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`
- IF you need more heat transfer: THEN you should rather add oil than turn the stove down. `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`
- IF you have stuck bits on the bottom of the pan: THEN you can add a spill of water to loosen them. `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`
- IF you are adding lemon juice and rosemary at the end: THEN use a bit less heat. `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`
- IF you are steaming the rosemary: THEN use a half-closed lid or a fat screen for 30 seconds to 1 minute, on medium to low heat. `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`
- IF you are cooking broccoli for frying: THEN salting it in advance makes the flavour intensify a lot. `[Context: Salt in layers discussion]`

**B. The "Roast First, Then Go for Doneness" Principle**
- IF you are cooking a dish: THEN you should always roast ingredients first to build flavour, and only then go for doneness. `[Context: Distinction between cooking and frying]`
- IF you close the lid completely: THEN this often builds up a light amount of pressure, which is enough to ruin crispiness quite instantly. `[Context: Distinction between cooking and frying]`

**C. The Conviction vs. Questioning Framework**
- IF you are cooking: THEN you need conviction in your pan that things will not immediately burn. `[Context: Conviction in the pan discussion]`
- AND/WHILE you are cooking: THEN you must always question what you are doing, reasoning about all the processes that are happening. `[Context: Marco Pierre White quote]`
- IF you are in a state of questioning: THEN the internal dialogue is based on "honest attention/focus on what is actually happening in the pan." `[Context: Internal dialogue description]`

**D. Handling Mistakes**

## 3. FAILURE MODES & EDGE CASES

- **Moisture loss (Chicken):**
    - IF the pan is overwhelmed by heat (too much food, not enough pan capacity): THEN the chicken cooks rather than fries, and too much moisture comes out. `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`
    - IF the chicken is flipped too soon: THEN the chicken cooks rather than fries, and too much moisture comes out. `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`
    - IF steam/heat is trapped (e.g., with a lid) during the initial searing: THEN the chicken cooks rather than fries, and too much moisture comes out. `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`
- **Boiling vs. Frying:**
    - IF there is too much fat/oil in the pan: THEN the food will start boiling instead of frying. `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`
    - IF the heat is too low: THEN the food will not fry properly (implied by the recommendation to add oil rather than turn the stove down). `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`
- **Loss of Crispiness:**
    - IF the lid is closed completely during the roasting phase: THEN the light pressure built up in the pan is enough to ruin crispiness quite instantly. `[Context: Distinction between cooking and frying]`
- **Overcooking:**
    - IF the sequence of adding ingredients is not correct: THEN ingredients will be overcooked/over-roasted. The sequence protects each ingredient's ideal state. `[Context: Sequencing discussion]`

## 4. TRADE-OFF ANALYSIS

- **High Heat vs. Gentleness (Conviction vs. Questioning):**
    - The text presents this as a productive tension, not a trade-off. The practitioner must hold both simultaneously: have the **conviction** to use high heat and trust the pan, while **constantly questioning** and observing what is happening. Failure occurs when one side dominates (e.g., blind conviction leading to burning, or excessive questioning leading to flipping too soon). `[Context: Entire interview, but especially the Marco Pierre White quotes]`

- **Fat Quantity (Too Little vs. Too Much):**
    - **Too little fat:** Heat transfer is inefficient; food sticks and burns.
    - **Too much fat:** The food boils rather than fries.
    - The "right amount" is a judgment call based on "honest attention," not a fixed measurement. The text provides no quantitative threshold, only the qualitative description of "not too much" and a "hood amount." `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`

- **Salt as Amplifier vs. Salt as Risk:**
    - **Amplifier:** Salt enhances the flavour of whatever aroma it is hitting. Salting in layers (at different stages) spotlights different elements of the dish. `[Context: Salt in layers discussion]`
    - **Risk:** The text explicitly recommends salting "a bit generously," which implies a risk of over-salting. The trade-off is accepted in pursuit of flavour.
    - The text does not address the consequences of over-salting or how to correct it. `[Context: Salt in layers discussion]`

- **Lid Use (Speed vs. Quality/Crispiness):**
    - **Lid ON (during onion roasting):** Uses trapped moisture to fasten the cooking of the onions. This is a pragmatic trade-off for speed. `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`
    - **Lid ON (during chicken searing):** Traps steam, causing the chicken to cook rather than fry, leading to moisture loss and loss of crispiness. This is a failure mode. `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`
    - **Lid ON (at the end, low heat):** Used to gently steam the rosemary without burning it. This is a desirable use of the lid. `[Context: Chicken dish walkthrough]`
