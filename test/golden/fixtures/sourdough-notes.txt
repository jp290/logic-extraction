Sourdough timing notes

A starter is ready when it has doubled within four to six hours of feeding at room temperature and smells sharp but not like nail polish. If it takes longer than eight hours, feed it twice a day for three days before baking with it.

Autolyse the flour and water for thirty minutes before adding the starter. The dough should feel shaggy at first and smooth after the first set of folds. Do four sets of stretch-and-folds, thirty minutes apart, then leave the dough alone.

Bulk fermentation is the step people get wrong. Watch the dough, not the clock: it is done when it has grown by about half, the surface is domed, and bubbles show along the sides of the container. In a warm kitchen at 26 °C this can take four hours; in a cold one at 20 °C it can take eight.

Shape gently and build tension on the surface without tearing it. Proof in the fridge overnight; the cold slows the yeast more than the bacteria, so the flavour gets more sour while the structure holds.

Bake in a preheated pot with the lid on for twenty minutes so the steam lets the loaf expand, then take the lid off and bake until the crust is deep brown. Pale bread is underbaked bread. Let it cool for an hour before cutting, because the crumb is still setting.

When a loaf comes out flat, ask which stage failed: a weak starter, an under-fermented bulk, or an over-proofed shape. Change one thing at a time on the next bake.
